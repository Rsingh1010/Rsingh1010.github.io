PokéLab: Machine Learning and Team Optimization November 2026

A web app that asks two questions about all 1,025 Pokémon: do their stats naturally fall into a few distinct kinds of build, and can math pick a stronger team of six than simple rules? The answers were no and yes, and the app shows the evidence for both.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • SciPy
  • PuLP
  • Streamlit
  • Plotly
  • pytest

How I built it

  • Built a cached, validated data pipeline over PokéAPI and Smogon usage stats covering 1,025 species, with documented cleaning rules and a pytest suite.
  • Tested K-Means and Gaussian mixture models against a stability rule set fixed before seeing results; no number of clusters from 3 to 8 passed (best bootstrap ARI 0.591 vs. 0.60 required), so the app reports a continuum, not archetypes.
  • Linked stats to competitive usage: total stats track usage (Spearman 0.51, 95% CI 0.44–0.58), while stat shape adds almost nothing on held-out data.
  • Formulated team building as an integer program in PuLP that maximizes type coverage, resistances, role coverage, and a stat floor under team rules; only 4% of 10,000 random teams met the rules and none matched the optimum.

Mean-Reversion Pairs Trading September 2026 – October 2026

Some stocks, like Coca-Cola and Pepsi, tend to move together. A classic trading idea is to bet that when their prices drift apart, they will come back together. I tested this carefully on 8 stock pairs: one pair passed the statistics, but it did not make money once trading costs were counted.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • statsmodels
  • yfinance

How I built it

  • Rebuilt an initial backtest after finding 5 flaws, including look-ahead bias in the hedge ratio, no train/test split, and pairs picked by correlation instead of cointegration.
  • Tested 8 candidate pairs with Engle-Granger cointegration and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction; one pair survived (KO–PEP, adjusted p = 0.036).
  • Ran transaction-cost sensitivity, statistical-power, and walk-forward stability checks that found no reliable edge, and closed the project as a documented negative result instead of tuning until it looked good.
